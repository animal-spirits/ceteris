Six ways to give a character a look without drawing a face

The same four generated people appear in each mock: a Brazilian finance minister, a Nigerian general, a Swedish central bank governor and a Turkish party leader. Every mock is deterministic from the character's seed and record, so replays and forks show the same thing.

1 of 6 · recommended

Press photos from your own model, printed as halftone

With a key connected (as in the flag studio), the app writes the prompt below, the model returns one photo per person, and the app prints it in the seat's press style. The image is stored by hash; it is regenerated from the first one each decade to age. This page cannot reach an image model, so the frames show the treatment on a soft placeholder. Drop any image, such as a portrait you generated elsewhere, onto a card to see it printed.

Real people never get a generated photo. The prompt always says "fictional", carries no real name, and the provider's own policy applies. Cost is one image per person per decade, roughly a few hundred images over a long run, only for people you open.

2 of 6 · offline fallback

Cut-paper silhouettes

A profile is one curve, which code can draw cleanly: forehead, brow, nose, lips, chin, hair and collar from the genome. Age lengthens the nose, softens the chin and thins the hair. Generals wear a peaked cap, everyone else is bare-headed.

3 of 6

Signatures

Each person gets a hand, a slant, a pressure and a flourish, drawn from the seed. The signature appears on decrees, treaties, banknotes and letters wherever their office signs.

Epithets and voice

A nickname is earned by a rule over the person's record and memory, so it changes when the run does. Each person also gets a phrase they repeat and a register for their speeches, which the chronicle uses (ADR 0078).

5 of 6

Intelligence file

The dossier signals of DESIGN 23.5 shown as a file your own service keeps. Hidden traits are redacted until there is evidence; each estimate shows its confidence and the record it rests on. Better intelligence (ADR 0153) unredacts more.

6 of 6

Personal effects

A flat lay of what is on the person's desk. Each object follows from the record: profession, office, age, habits drawn at creation, and what they lived through.
