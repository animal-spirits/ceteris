Hohmann: a space companion game to Ceteris

Brainstorm, 29 Oct 2026 · working title · follows the city-companion pattern (separate game, versioned interface files) · no ADRs

Pitch

A hard-science game about building a real economy beyond Earth from about 2030 to 2200, where Earth is not a backdrop but a Ceteris run. You run a launch-and-settlement company (or a national agency). Every price in space is an Earth price plus the rocket equation. The question the whole game turns on is a real one: at what launch price does it pay to make anything off Earth?

Space is the friendliest genre for a no-art, AI-built game. It is black, schematic and numeric by nature; planet textures are public domain from NASA; the best-loved games in the niche (Aurora 4X, Children of a Dead Earth, High Frontier) are one-person projects or have programmer art. Today's models can write most of the code. Balance and fun still need a human playing it, and that, not code, is the bottleneck.

The gap (interpolation)

No game combines three things: an honest solar system (delta-v, windows, the rocket equation), a real Earth economy that sets the prices and the money, and the century-scale question of whether off-world industry ever pays. Terra Invicta has the first and half of the second; High Frontier has the first; Distant Worlds has a self-running economy in a fantasy setting. Ceteris already has the second, and its structural-gravity trade model extends naturally to space: trade cost between two nodes becomes a function of delta-v, transfer time and window wait.

Core mechanics

  • Map as a delta-v graph. Nodes are places where things stop: LEO, GEO, Earth-Moon L1, low lunar orbit, lunar polar sites, Mars orbit, Phobos, Mars sites, near-Earth asteroids, Ceres, Jupiter's moons. Edges carry delta-v, transfer time and an aerobrake flag. Planet positions come from JPL ephemerides, so windows open and close on their real dates.
  • Prices from the rocket equation. The delivered price of a kilogram at a node is its source price times the propellant mass ratio along the cheapest path, plus vehicle amortization. One formula prices everything, and every number in the game can be traced to it.
  • Water is money. Water is propellant, air and radiation shielding. Lunar polar ice, Phobos, carbonaceous asteroids and Ceres are rated by accessibility, as in Aurora.
  • Settlements by closure. Each habitat has a closure ratio per resource (water, oxygen, food, spares). The remaining mass must be imported every month at the delivered price. Population, births and morale follow from that and from the habitat's volume and radiation dose.
  • Learning curves, not a tech tree. Costs fall with cumulative output (Wright's law), as in Ceteris 3.0. Milestones switch on when production and investment cross thresholds. Propulsion stays grounded: chemical, nuclear thermal (NERVA test data), electric, solar sails.
  • Firms and agencies are agents. As in Distant Worlds, private freighters and producers act on prices. The player sets the strategy for one company or agency.
  • Law and politics. The Outer Space Treaty, US 2015 and Luxembourg 2017 resource laws, Artemis Accords against the Chinese-Russian lunar station bloc. Late in the game, settlements can ask for self-rule.
  • Risk from data. Launch and landing failure rates by vehicle maturity, from the GCAT launch log. About half of all Mars missions so far have failed, and early players should feel that.

The central number

Water delivered to Earth-Moon L1 (hydrolox engines, exhaust speed 4.4 km/s, no tank mass, no reuse; simplified on purpose). From Earth, it costs the launch price P times the mass ratio for LEO to L1 (3.77 km/s, ratio 2.36). From the lunar surface, it costs the extraction cost c times the ratio for surface to L1 (about 2.5 km/s, ratio 1.77). Lunar water wins only when c is below about 1.33 P.

Cheap launch makes off-Earth industry near Earth harder to justify, not easier. The further out you go (Mars, the Belt), the more the ratio favours local production. That tension, set by a launch price that comes from the Ceteris run, is the game's strategic spine.

Interface with Ceteris

Same pattern as the city companion: two versioned files, no space maths at Ceteris runtime, and a Ceteris run that imports a summary is marked extended profile.

FileDirectionCarries
Earth contextCeteris → gameLaunch price, metal and energy prices, cost of capital, space budgets by state, treaty blocs, demand for space services by income, debris state (2.0 model); frozen or a time series
Space summaryGame → CeterisExports to Earth by good (services, tourism, data, later metals or power), launch demand, capital invested by owner country, debris added, context hash

The game also ships its own data-built 2025 Earth context, so it never waits on Ceteris 2.0. Ceteris-side cost: a context exporter and a summary importer, about one FTE-month, after 2.0's space work exists.

No art, no team: how it looks

  • Schematic first. The main screen is the delta-v map as a transit diagram, with flows as moving particles. Pure vector, procedural.
  • Orbital view. Lines and dots on true ephemerides; planets from NASA imagery (public domain) and Solar System Scope textures (CC BY 4.0).
  • Habitats as blueprints. Procedural cross-sections from module stacks: torus, cylinder, buried lava-tube. No characters, no portraits, the same rule as Ceteris (ADRs 0183 to 0187).
  • Mission patches. Generated from a grammar (orbit glyph, body, crew count, Roman numeral). A cheap way to give missions identity.
  • Stack. Reuse the Ceteris app: Tauri, the same design system, a Rust sim crate with the same determinism lints. Bevy only if a 3D orbital view grows beyond lines and textured spheres.

Feasibility on today's models

TierScopeOwner FTE-months with AI
0 · Paper prototypeDelta-v graph, rocket-equation prices, one company, text UI; answers "is the central tension fun?"1-2
1 · Minimal gameSettlements with closure, mission planner, frozen Earth context, events, save and load, the schematic map6-9
2 · CoupledTime-series context from Ceteris, summary back, politics and treaties, AI rival firms and agencies, orbital view+6-9
3 · Full releaseCampaign balance, tutorial, onboarding, polish, localization+12 or more; balance is the bottleneck

What today's models do well here: orbital mechanics (patched conics, Lambert solvers after Izzo 2015), the economic core, UI code, data pipelines, tests, procedural vector art. What they do poorly: judge whether a decision is interesting, find dominant strategies before players do, keep a large codebase coherent without written specs (the Ceteris spec and ADR workflow already covers that), and write hundreds of events in one consistent voice without editing.

Biggest risk: this is a third project next to Ceteris and the city companion, for one person. Tier 0 is cheap enough to try any time; anything past it competes with Ceteris for the same hours.

If models get better (speculative, not planned on)

  • Playtesting agents. Models that play thousands of campaigns and report dominant strategies and dead ends would remove the main bottleneck for Tier 3. Scripted bots can do part of this today.
  • Consistent generated 3D. Habitat and ship meshes in one style would allow a realistic look without artists. Today it would be the stylized schematic.
  • Large codebase coherence. If a model can hold a full game codebase and its specs at once, Tier 3 moves from studio scale to owner scale.
  • Narration. Event text written from state, cached by state hash so runs stay deterministic. Narration only, never causal, consistent with rejecting LLM diplomacy for Ceteris.
  • Design rule: each improvement should upgrade the game, never be a dependency. Every tier above ships without it.

From science fiction: art without artists

  • 1970s NASA space colony paintings. Rick Guidice and Don Davis painted the Stanford torus and O'Neill cylinders for NASA Ames. As NASA works they are public domain, so they can be loading screens with credit, at no cost.
  • NASA Graphics Standards Manual (1975). Danne and Blackburn's grid, rules and type. The mission-control UI can follow it closely, and it is pure layout, so no art is needed.
  • Ron Cobb's semiotic symbols for Alien. A geometric icon language for ship systems. Copy the idea, not the icons: a small grammar of shapes (circle = habitat, triangle = hazard, bar = flow) generates every icon procedurally.
  • Kubrick's 2001. Clean white interiors and flat screen graphics. A light theme for agencies.
  • Used future (Alien, The Expanse). Scuffed panels, stencil labels, hand-painted signs. A dark theme for companies and Belt settlements, made with shaders and noise, not drawings.
  • Technical manuals and cutaways (Haynes, Jane's, Bonestell's cross-sections). Habitat blueprints as the settlement view.
  • Soviet cosmonautics posters. Bold diagonals and flat colour, as style reference only, since many are still under copyright.

From science fiction: flavour

  • Encyclopedia epigraphs. In the manner of Foundation's Encyclopedia Galactica, each chapter of the chronicle opens with an entry written from the end of the run, built from its own state ("Occator, founded 2051, was the first...").
  • Mission patches generated per mission, the way NASA crews design them: body, orbit, crew names, numerals.
  • Names from the founders' own history. Settlements and ships are named by the founding firm or state from their own explorers, scientists and places, from Wikidata, by the same rule as Ceteris names.
  • Radio voice. Event text in the clipped style of mission loops and capcom calls, with real light-lag before replies from Mars.
  • Mars time. A sol is 24 h 39 min. Settlement clocks drift against Earth's, and shift rotas show it.
  • Water talk. Prices, debts and wages quoted in tonnes of water at the outer settlements, as Belter fiction imagines, because water really is the propellant.

Picks (Antonio, 29 Oct 2026)

  • Seats: all three (company, agency, settlement governor) from the start. This roughly triples balancing work before the first playable, so Tier 1 grows from 6-9 toward 9-13 owner-months.
  • Realism: known and plausible by default, plus an opt-in speculative dial, every item flagged in the UI.
  • Timing: parked indefinitely. This page is the design note.
