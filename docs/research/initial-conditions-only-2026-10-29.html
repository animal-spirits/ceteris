Answer

Yes. The world is data on 1 January 2000 and a generator after it. The generator reads state, parameters and its own random streams, never the calendar, so the equations that run in 2001 are the ones that run in 2101. There is no "after the data runs out" because the data runs out on day one.

Real history 2000-2024 becomes one draw the model should find plausible, not the script. The shock tape survives only as a lab instrument, "replay history": a saved set of interventions (COVID seeded in Wuhan in December 2019, the observed policy rates, ERA5 weather) that calibration and natural-experiment tests apply on purpose and log as edits. A default run never sees it.

The design is mostly there already: 13.6 keeps crises and recessions endogenous, 0229 fires rescues on simulated stress, 0051 has a hazard library. What has to change is the default of 0210 (tape then rules), the "tape to 2024, held after" rows in 0210, 0229 and 0233, and the three tape modes of 13.1.

The rule: calendar-blind

After t0 a run may read only state, parameters and counter-keyed random draws. Nothing indexed by date. Three things look like dates but are state:

  • Month of year for seasons, harvests and weather.
  • Ages and durations (pop cohorts, bond maturities, a peg's age).
  • Commitments in force at t0, if D2 keeps them: schedules already adopted on 1 Jan 2000 (Uruguay Round phase-ins, the end of textile quotas in 2005, euro notes in 2002) and projects under construction. These are the world's state on that day; a 2024 start carries its own.

Test. Relabel the start year of the same pack (2000 to 2100) and the golden hash must not move, apart from labels. One CI test catches any date-keyed input that slips in.

Branches stay comparable. State-dependent hazards are drawn by thinning: one uniform per (hazard, cell, tick) from the counter RNG, and the event fires if it falls below the rate. Two branches share the uniforms, so an event happens in both unless an intervention moved its rate across the draw (maximal coupling; keeps 14.4 common random numbers). "No pandemic" becomes an edit of the run's own event, not of history.

Three jobs, three methods. Only the second uses replay, and only as a measuring instrument.

What to expect. Unconditional bands will be wide by 2009 and 2020, as they should be: the model did not know about Lehman or COVID, and neither did the WEO forecasts it is benchmarked against (0052), so the short-horizon comparison is fair. Rare-event frequencies need large ensembles; 0107 already sends events below 10% to the lab profile with K of 256 or more.

2024 is just another origin. 15.4 already builds packs for 2000, 2005, 2010 and 2015 from one pipeline. A run from 2000 past 2024 and a run from a 2024 pack use identical code; only initial conditions differ. 0103's drift is the pre-origin trend, so it is state too (a 2024 origin needs a trend window that skips 2020-21).

Costs. One CI test (year relabel), a hazard registry with thinning (most of it is 0051), a pandemic spillover map, a replay loader that turns the tape tables into logged interventions (the existing tape reader, reused), and DESIGN 13 and 15 rewrites. No new free behavioural parameters: hazard parameters are fitted on catalogues and go in the register as data-estimated. Unconditional hindcast scores will look worse than conditional ones; that gap is reported, not hidden.

D1. What does a default run read after 1 Jan 2000?

The whole proposal rests on this.

Decides whether China's WTO accession, EU 2004 enlargement and projects under way happen in the default run.
