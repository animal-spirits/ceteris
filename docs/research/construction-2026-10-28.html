Building things in Ceteris

What can be built, at what grain, how a project goes from order to working capacity, what it changes, and how that compares with Victoria 3, Europa Universalis IV and V, and Hearts of Iron III and IV. A proposal: nothing here is an ADR yet.

Short answer

five points
No buildings, capital stocks.Ordinary capacity is a continuous stock per enterprise cell (province, sector, ownership), split by asset class. Only lumpy, map-visible things are named projects: corridors, ports, power plants, dams, pipelines, mines.
Construction is a real sector.Every project buys a bundle of construction services, machinery and equipment from province markets. The construction sector's capacity, labour, cement and steel are the bottleneck, priced, as in the data. No construction points.
Time to build is staged.Orders pass a permit lag, then spend over an Erlang-shaped schedule by asset class (machinery in quarters, structures in years, nuclear near a decade). Work in progress sits on the owner's balance sheet.
Coefficients lock at order.A plant's input mix is chosen when it is ordered (putty-clay, ADR 0020). Change the technology and you build new capacity. Stranded assets and overruns follow.
Fits 1.0.The pipeline lands with investment in P5, named projects and endogenous infrastructure in P8, validation in P9 against India's Golden Quadrilateral and China's 2009 stimulus. Seats and play verbs are 1.x minors; military building is 2.0.

What can be built

two grains

Ordinary capital follows the national-accounts asset split, because that is how the data come (PWT, EU KLEMS, the ICIO investment column). Lumpy assets that change the map or the network are named projects with a location, a size and a schedule. The threshold between the two is a per-class size cutoff in the pack.

How a project goes from order to capacity

quarterly step, monthly spend

Schedule

Each asset class a has a mean build time T_a and a shape k_a. A linear chain of k_a stages gives an Erlang-distributed completion time with O(1) state per stage, the same trick DESIGN 19.4 uses for other lags.

W[s](t+1) = W[s](t) + flow[s-1](t) - flow[s](t) flow[s](t) = (k_a / T_a) * W[s](t) * min(1, delivered / ordered) K(t+1) = (1 - delta_a) K(t) + flow[k_a](t)

The min term is the link to rationing (ADR 0020): if the construction market cannot deliver, stages slow. Work in progress W counts in the goods invariant of 4.6 like the input stocks of 0170.

Cost

The bundle per asset class comes from the ICIO investment column by country (GFCF by product), so Chinese structures are cement-heavy and US ones are not. The construction sector is an ordinary ICIO sector, local in the gravity kernel with high frictions, and imports construction services only at P141 (0086).

Its own capacity grows through the same pipeline (machinery is quick to add) and through labour: construction labour is a skill-group market (0105), so a boom raises construction wages, pulls migrants (0174 Gulf workers) and slows everything else that needs crews.

Failure modes

  • Overrun and delay drawn per named project from reference classes (Flyvbjerg et al.: rail about +45%, roads about +20% on average), keyed by the run seed (0160).
  • A cash call the owner cannot meet pauses the project. Paused work in progress decays at its depreciation rate.
  • Abandonment writes off the work in progress through 0112. Unfinished housing after 2008 in Spain and after 2021 in China are the test cases.

Cost per tick

Two staged asset groups (structures, equipment) times three stages per active enterprise cell, plus housing and public capital per province. At roughly 300,000 active cells that is about 14 MB. The update is quarterly and linear; it is negligible next to the 30 ms trade step.

Who decides and how you play it

split with the companies thread

Who decides

The companies thread owns the investor: its objective, the Tobin-q logit of 7.4, financing, SOE weights (0171), foreign owners (0104, 0041). This note owns what an order turns into. The interface is one record:

Order { owner: OwnerId, province, asset, size, coefficients, schedule, soe_override: bool }

The queue returns cash calls, completed capacity, and delay or overrun outcomes to the owner. SOE orders can carry negative expected value when the state's investment lever overrides the return rule; the queue accepts them.

Verbs

  • Lab, 1.0. Edits ex ante or ex post: add or cancel a project anywhere, change its schedule, shock construction capacity, switch the historical project tape on or off.
  • Corridor planner seat, 1.x (0160): build one link, scored on market access against realized cost.
  • Steward, 1.3. Public investment budget by class and province, SOE investment, concessions and PPPs, permitting speed, investment tax credits. Private capacity is never placed by click; it responds to prices, credit and incentives.
  • Planned economy seat, 1.x. Direct placement of state capacity in countries where the state owns it, which is what Victoria 3's command economy approximates.
  • Views. A project map mode (named projects as pins with schedule bars), a construction-capacity heat map, and the bottleneck lens (0155) showing which project waits on which input.

Historical project tape: for the 2000-2024 hindcast, named projects that really happened (Three Gorges, Qinghai-Tibet railway, Golden Quadrilateral, Nord Stream) can come from a tape, like policy in 1.0 (ADR 0136), or be left endogenous. The scorecard reports both lanes.

What we take

  • Victoria 3: construction as a sector that consumes goods. Ceteris already has construction as an ICIO sector; making it the binding constraint is the borrow. Also the public and private queue split, as state budgets against private orders.
  • HOI4: a per-project ceiling on parallel effort. Here it is the minimum build time T_a/k_a per stage: more money cannot build a dam in a year.
  • HOI3: serial-run gearing, as Wright's-law learning on repeated named projects of one class in one country (Korea's reactor fleet, China's high-speed rail), lab-off until it passes ablation.
  • EU5: roads raising control, which is ADR 0163 state reach.
  • EU4: great projects, as named megaprojects in the pack with their real 2000 status.

What we leave

  • Building levels and slots as the unit of capital. The data are stocks by asset; levels would need an invented conversion and would make capital lumpy where it is not.
  • Fixed costs and fixed build times. Cost and speed are outcomes of the construction market.
  • Switching production methods in place. It contradicts putty-clay and removes stranded assets, one of the main transition questions.
  • Click-to-place private capacity. In the lab the player is not the investor; in seats only the state places what the state owns.
  • Monarch-point development. Development is the sum of what got built and what it does.

The net difference: in every Paradox game a project is a progress bar with a fixed price. In Ceteris it is a claim on scarce crews and materials, financed by someone who can run out of money, locked into a technology at order, and late more often than not. That is where the interesting outcomes live: stimulus that hits a capacity wall, a boom that strands half-built towers, a transition slowed by turbine and grid lead times.

Tests the ADR would carry

  • With T_a at one quarter, k_a at one and no permit lag, results equal the current 7.4 rule bit for bit.
  • Work in progress and capital close the goods and money invariants every tick.
  • A toy stimulus at full construction capacity raises construction prices and delays completions; with slack it does not.
  • The steady-state 2000 pipeline holds still for 100 years with no shocks (0089).

Data

  • PWT 10 capital by asset and depreciation (CC BY 4.0); EU KLEMS where finer.
  • ICIO investment column by product, already in the pack.

  • World Bank Doing Business construction-permit days, 2004-2019 (CC BY 4.0), for the permit lag.
  • IAEA PRIS for reactor build times, and reference-class overrun data, both with licences to check under ADR 0005.

Decisions for you

recommendation first
1Grain: capital stocks plus named lumpy projects.Alternatives: Victoria-style building levels everywhere, or everything continuous with no named projects. Stocks match the data; named projects keep the map legible.
2Staged time to build by asset class, slowed by rationing.Alternative: keep the instant 7.4 rule. Time to build is what makes stimulus, booms and the energy transition behave as observed.
3Construction capacity is the construction sector's priced output.Alternative: a separate construction-points pool. Points would duplicate a sector that already exists in the IO table.
4Coefficients lock at order.Alternative: at completion. Order is what firms commit to; completion-time choice would remove the stranded-asset risk of long projects.
5The player places only state capacity; private capacity responds to incentives.Alternative: any capacity by click in seats. That would turn Steward into a planner of every economy.
