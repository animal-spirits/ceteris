# P5 E3: spike of RFC 0341 D1 and D2, 2026-11-10

Fixture (`small_world`), frictionless, anchor on (ADRs 0338, 0340), from the blessed reference
state, ntb on every border. Single-run fixture numbers; estimates, not calibration. Spike code
(not merged): `docs/research/spikes/p5-e3/d1d2-spike-2026-11-10.patch`, harness
`crates/ceteris-econ/tests/p5_e3_d1d2.rs` in it (env KS = kappa_s, PS = pay on sales, NTB, T).

## What was built

- D1: `PriceAnchor::kappa_s`, the stock term `-kappa_s (S - s_g D) / max(s_g D, floor_g)` in
  `reprice_anchored` on coverage cells (added to kappa2, which stays 0).
- D2: `EconParams::pay_on_sales`. E2 ships output to the market without the SALE payment and
  adds it to the enterprise's unpaid consignment. After T2b the market pays `p` per unit sold
  (`Markets::sales`), this tick's output first, then the backlog, both pro rata; the backlog of
  a traded cell is capped at the market's stock, that of a local cell perishes.

## Why D2 fails

D2 does what it says: the p24 pops stop losing (their money sits at 0) and the loss lands on the
p24 enterprises. But the RFC's premise, that the enterprise loss cap and write-off (ADR 0112)
bound the loss, is false. The write-off moves the excess over the cap onto the pops' positive
money, never below zero; what the pops cannot cover stays with the enterprise, unbounded. And
nothing stops an enterprise in overdraft from paying wages and placing import orders. So the
same money leaves the island, now through enterprise overdraft instead of the market dividend,
and the world dies at the same ticks.

## The ntb 3 residual, traced

p24 is the only province of its country (no other province of the pool). At ntb 3 the island
loses about 7.5e6 a tick through the whole run (p24 pops -2.2e9 by t300, kappa_s 0 or 0.05)
while its market's cash, sampled, is near 0. With pay on sales on, the pops end each tick at 0
and the enterprises carry the same amount (-2.3e9 to -2.6e9 by t300). So the residual is the
island's external deficit: imports at the wedge exceed exports every tick, and whichever account
is allowed to go negative finances it. Under the default rule that is the market's small signed
dividend accumulated over hundreds of ticks; under D2 it is the enterprise overdraft.

## Conclusion

Both the x10 sink and the ntb 3 residual are one mechanism: an account that may hold negative
money and still spend (the pops through the signed market dividend, or the enterprises through
overdraft) finances an unbounded trade deficit. D2 moves the negative balance; it does not bound
it. A fix must bound spending against money (a budget or credit limit on the account that pays
for imports), or close the balance of payments by a price (a country price level or exchange
rate that responds to the external position). Either is a design decision beyond RFC 0341.

## Follow-up: credit limit on traded input orders (same day)

Antonio chose a credit limit on spending (2026-11-10) and asked that the record say the model
should have both a credit limit and a balance-of-payments price; the price closure is deferred
to a later RFC. Spike (`docs/research/spikes/p5-e3/credit-limit-spike-2026-11-10.patch`, env CL):
each enterprise's traded input spending at E3 is scaled down to its cash plus CL times its loss
cap (ADR 0112), with pay on sales on. Wages are not bounded: they stay in the province.

**No-go.** The limit does close the leak: positive balances stay at 2.5e9-3.3e9 instead of
tripling, the island's enterprises stop at their caps and its output goes to 0, and at ntb 1
with CL of at least 1 the steady state is untouched. But the three mainland countries (no
enterprise near its limit) die anyway: the index climbs from 1.04 at t40 to 1.37 at t90, then
output falls to 0 by t140. The island supplies 1-3% of goods 0, 3, 4 and nothing else, so its
idling is not a supply loss to them. Without pay on sales the same limit (CL 1) runs like the
baseline (index 1.04 to t300, dies t500). So with the money leak closed, a second failure shows,
earlier: pay on sales plus bounded orders raise the mainland's excess demand. A working
capital limit (CL 0) deadlocks even without a shock: inputs must be paid before the sales that
fund them.

Open, not traced (two approaches tried, stopped as the process rule says): why the mainland's
excess demand rises from t40 under pay on sales with bounded orders. Candidate: the earlier
root cause (world-mean excess demand not priced by the anchored rule, plus stock-target input
orders), now unmasked.
