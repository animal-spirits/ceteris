Ceteris feasibility note · 29 Oct 2026 · 2.0+ war companion

Brigade Fight Companion

Can one owner, no artists, with AI writing the code, build a battle game in the line of Total War and its relatives, plugged into Ceteris war? Short answer: yes for a 2D operational-tactical game on real terrain with NATO symbols, no for a 3D Total War or Combat Mission class game.

Verdict, assuming today's models

Feasible: a separate program that takes one Ceteris front segment for a few hours to a day, breaks the engaged brigades into companies and batteries, and lets you fight it on a 2D map generated from real elevation, land cover and roads, in pausable real time with order latency through HQs. Close in spirit to Command Ops 2, Armored Brigade and Flashpoint Campaigns, all of which were built by very small teams. About 8 to 12 owner FTE-months for a playable version; a watch-only replay of Ceteris battles comes first at 1.5 to 2.5.

Not feasible: 3D battles with individual soldiers and vehicles (Total War, Combat Mission, WARNO, Gates of Hell). Infantry animation, vehicle models and effects are what studios spend their artists on; procedural generation does not replace them today. Studio scale, 150 to 500+ person-months.

Hard part: the AI opponent, not the graphics. It is the weak point of every game on the list, including the large ones.

When: after war stage W5 (theatre view and battle planner, ADR 0166), so 2.0-beta or later. Nothing needs doing now.

Decided (Antonio, 29 Oct 2026), then parked: Command Ops / Armored Brigade family; 2.5D diorama with tokens carrying NATO symbols (adds 3 to 5 FTE-months, so a playable version is about 11 to 17); results enter Ceteris as a quantile of the resolver, clamped 5 to 95 per cent.

How it plugs into Ceteris

Same architecture as the city companion: a separate program, two versioned files, no battle code at Ceteris runtime. ADR 0149's "no continuous battle view" stays true for Ceteris itself; the companion is outside it and runs in the extended profile.

Coupling modes

  • Quantile (recommended default). The resolver already has a distribution for that segment-day (its keyed lognormal noise). Your result is scored and mapped to a quantile of that distribution, clamped to 5 to 95 per cent, and Ceteris applies the resolver's outcome at that quantile. Skill moves you within the plausible range; it cannot make a company of tanks beat a corps. The world's statistics and the nesting test stay intact.
  • Raw import. The companion's losses and control replace the resolver's. Truer to play, but a good player beats the resolver every time, so the run leaves the lab profile and carries a flag in its hash.
  • Replay only. The companion renders Ceteris's own hourly log (0209) at company grain. No influence on the sim; cheapest, and the first thing to build either way.

Grain and time

A Ceteris segment is 5 to 20 km and fights for days. A companion battle covers one segment for a window of hours inside a day, at company and battery grain, disaggregated from the brigade by its 0146 template. To avoid a prompt every day of a war, offer "fight" only for operations planned in the battle planner (0166, L1 to L2) or when the player asks.

Generation from real data

Terrain

  • Elevation: Copernicus GLO-30 (30 m). Line of sight and slope at company scale need nothing finer.
  • Land cover: ESA WorldCover (10 m) for forest, crops, built-up, water.
  • Roads, rivers, bridges, rail: OpenStreetMap (ODbL, fine for an open project).
  • Built-up blocks: Overture or OSM footprints where present, GHSL density otherwise, rolled back toward 2000 with GHSL epochs.
  • From Ceteris: fortification and mine fields per cell, season, weather.
  • All the same sources the city companion uses; one terrain pipeline serves both.

Units

  • The brigade's template (0146) splits into companies and batteries by authorised counts and fill.
  • Each of the ~45 equipment classes becomes a weapon system: range bands, firepower against soft, armoured and air, protection, mobility, signature.
  • Vintage band sets sensors and protection; readiness, cohesion and supply carry over as starting state.
  • Names from the 0166 naming grammars; symbols from the 0166 NATO set with Ceteris amplifiers.
  • No per-soldier data is needed at this grain, which is why open sources are enough.

AI opponents

The biggest risk. Every game in the table above gets criticised for its AI, and attackers are much harder to write than defenders. Proposed layers:

  • HQ planner. Reuse the 0147 delegated planner one echelon down: pick objectives from influence maps (terrain value, threat, cover, routes), assign companies, set phase lines. Re-plans on events, not every tick.
  • Company behaviour. Small state machines per posture (attack, defend, delay, screen, withdraw) with utility scores for firing positions and routes. Suppression and cohesion can override orders, as in Close Combat.
  • Search where it pays. In WEGO mode, a shallow search over a handful of candidate plans per turn, scored by fast rollouts of the battle core. Too slow for real time at every level.
  • Tuning by self-play. Headless AI-versus-AI batches give win rates per scenario and expose degenerate plans. This is also the owner's playtest substitute.

Expect the defender AI to be decent within the first playable version and the attacker AI to take most of the second tier.

Determinism

  • Fixed step in integer seconds (10 s in the mock); every draw keyed by (context rng key, entity, step, purpose), as in 0145 and 0209.
  • The order log is the only input; real-time play only decides at which step an order enters. Replays and verification follow.
  • Pathfinding and planners with fixed iteration counts and ID tie-breaks; transcendental maths through a pure Rust libm so results match across platforms.
  • Battle core as a Rust crate that no Ceteris sim crate depends on; Ceteris ships only the context exporter, result importer and verifier.

For scale: the whole war build (W0 to W10) is already 45 to 70 part-time months, and 1.0 lands between late 2029 and 2031. T1 competes with the war stages it depends on, which is why it belongs after W5.

Separate note: if models are much better in a few years (not assumed above)
  • Art. Mesh generation, automatic rigging and motion generation could make vehicles and infantry usable without artists. That is the one change that would move realistic 3D from studio scale toward something like 30 to 60 owner-months. Consistency, level of detail and performance with hundreds of units remain the open questions.
  • AI opponents. A model acting as the enemy commander, issuing orders every few game minutes into the same order log as the player. Determinism survives because its orders are logged like yours; replays never call the model again. Cost and latency per battle would have to fall a lot for real time.
  • Voice and reports. Spoken orders and radio reports in the Radio Commander style become cheap, which makes the "reports only" mode a real difficulty setting.
  • Code throughput. T1 and T2 shrink, but owner time shifts to design review and playtesting, which models help with only through headless self-play.
