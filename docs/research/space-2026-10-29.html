Space in Ceteris

Brainstorm, 29 Oct 2026 · main e2cce80 · touches DESIGN 7.1, 23.7, 23.9; ADRs 0051, 0075, 0087, 0136, 0145, 0153, 0175, 0203, 0206. No ADRs written.

Verdict

Space fits Ceteris as infrastructure and as a very concentrated industry, not as a frontier to settle. Within 2000-2100 almost all of its economic weight is on Earth: navigation timing, communications, imagery, and the launch and satellite firms that supply them. Those all have data and can be scored. Off-world economies cannot be scored against anything, so under ADR 0136 they never earn a major and stay as extended-profile scenarios.

Most of the hooks already exist: the telecom layer (0075), synthetic satellite observables (0087), satellites as an intelligence source (0153), space weather (0175), granular firms (0203), named projects (0206) and the 23.7 capability milestone "satellites and space launch". The plan below threads space through them in four steps, with one new mechanism that has no home yet: orbital debris as a commons.

The sector itself is small. Launch plus satellite manufacturing is a few tens of billions a year; most of the $613 bn is downstream (broadcast, ground equipment, GNSS devices). What space is worth to the economy is its role as an input to almost everything else, which is how Ceteris should carry it.

Launch industry

  • Structure. In 2000 the commercial GEO market was split among Arianespace, ILS (Proton), Sea Launch, Boeing and Lockheed. By 2025 one firm flew about half the world's launches and most of the mass. This is the clearest granular-firm case in the economy: Falcon 9 reuse (first reflight 2017) was one firm's productivity shock that moved a world price.
  • Cost model. Wright's law on cumulative flights per vehicle family, plus a discrete reuse step. Demand comes from constellation replacement, government programmes and crewed flights. Two to four parameters.
  • Launch sites are provinces. Kourou, Cape Canaveral, Vandenberg, Starbase, Jiuquan, Wenchang, Sriharikota, Tanegashima, Plesetsk, Vostochny, Mahia. Baikonur is Kazakh territory leased to Russia, which is a ready test case for 0115 territory partitions.
  • Trade policy shocks in the window. The US moved commercial satellites onto the ITAR munitions list in 1999 and back out in 2014, and its share of the commercial satellite market fell in between. After February 2022 Russia lost the Western launch market (OneWeb moved off Soyuz). Both are tariff-like wedges the 8.5 trade-policy block can already carry.
  • Monopoly prices. NASA paid Russia per seat to the ISS between the Shuttle's retirement (2011) and Crew Dragon (2020), and the price rose several-fold over that time. That series is bilateral-monopoly bargaining with data.

State programmes and prestige

Budgets are data (NASA historical tables are public domain; ESA, ISRO and JAXA publish; CNSA and Roscosmos are estimates). In 1.0 they are government spending lines in O84 and M72 from the policy tape, nothing more.

Prestige is tempting and weakly evidenced. There is no solid estimate of what a crewed launch or a lunar landing does to approval or international standing. Recommendation: milestones (Shenzhou 5 in 2003, Chandrayaan-3 in 2023, Chang'e 6 far-side samples in 2024) become chronicle events and objectives in the game layer from 2.0, with no causal prestige term in the lab. One diplomacy signal does have data: the Artemis Accords (from 2020) against the China-Russia lunar station partners. Signatory lists are a bloc-alignment observable for 2.0 diplomacy.

Military space (2.0, with the war ADRs)

  • Intelligence. Imagery and signals satellites raise intelligence quality in 0153. Coverage is a theatre state updated daily, not an hourly unit (0145, 0209).
  • Dependencies. Precision munitions and drones (23.7 milestone) need positioning and satellite comms. Jamming or loss lowers their Lanchester modifier. Ukraine since 2022 is the reference case for jamming, spoofing and commercial constellations at war.
  • Counterspace. Kinetic anti-satellite tests: China 2007, US 2008, India 2019, Russia 2021. Each made debris that threatens the tester's own satellites too, which is a built-in deterrent the debris model below captures for free. Non-kinetic options (jamming, cyber, dazzling) carry no debris and are the common ones.
  • Firms at war. Starlink terminals and commercial imagery in Ukraine make a private firm a strategic actor. That is 0203's political-actor role, at 2.0.
  • Data. Secure World Foundation, Global Counterspace Capabilities (annual, free); CSIS Space Threat Assessment.

Orbital debris as a commons

This is the one genuinely new mechanism. It is a renewable-resource problem with open access, which economists have modelled well (Adilov, Alexander and Cunningham 2015, 2018; Rao, Burgess and Kaffine 2020 in PNAS, who estimate that an orbital-use fee could raise the long-run value of the industry several-fold). Per altitude shell k (four to six shells), each month:

S' = launches − retirements − α·S·(S+D) active satellites D' = retirements·(1 − disposal) + β·S·(S+D) + γ·D² + ASAT fragments − drag_k·D debris fee = policy lever per satellite-year (8.5-style wedge on launch demand)

Cost is a few multiplies per shell per tick. Parameters: collision rates α, β, γ and drag per shell, from the ESA Space Environment Report and published fits. Kessler syndrome is the case where γ·D² outruns drag; in a 100-year run it is a tail outcome. The model can produce it but no data can confirm it, so it runs as a scenario with an uncertainty band, never as a forecast. Recorded events to replay: Fengyun-1C (2007), Iridium-Cosmos (2009), Cosmos 1408 (2021).

Under 0136 a major must have its own scorecard. An off-world economy has no observations to score, so "Earth stops being closed" is not a major. Space never gets its own version; it rides on the majors whose scorecards it can join.

Decisions (Antonio, 29 Oct 2026)

  • No space major. Space is a 2.0+ feature, threaded through 2.0 and 3.0 as in the slotting table; 1.0 stays a pure economic simulator. Settlements stay mods.
  • Space becomes its own good at 3.0, as an emerging sector under C30. Before that the inspector shows satellite-account shares.
  • The debris model enters at 2.0 in the extended profile, together with counterspace. It reaches the lab at 3.0 only if the hindcast fits.
