What Paradox does on the map, and what Ceteris can take

Paradox games carry a lot of their charm in small cues: a map that changes character as you zoom out, names painted across the land, a selection that glows, notices you can fly to, a pause you can see from across the room. Twenty-eight such cues below, taken as patterns, not assets. Seventeen fit the pure-simulator 1.0; the rest wait for war or for the 2.0 game layer.

Ground rules

How a Paradox cue gets into Ceteris

The pattern, never the assetParadox's cues are built for a player who owns a country. Ceteris borrows the mechanism (semantic zoom, coalesced notices) and draws it in the Modern Atlas register of ADR 0137.
1.0 cues help read the runUnder ADR 0240 a cue sits on the 1.0 path only when it helps a user read, edit or compare runs. Voice, drama and judgement wait for 2.0+.
Nothing fires on a dateEvery pin, notice and sting comes from a detector event in the simulated run (ADRs 0049, 0241). No scripted event windows, no options to click.
No player, so no judgement coloursParadox paints numbers green when they help you. 1.0 has no "you", so colour shows direction only (idea 17).

    Plate 2

    Tooltips that lock, numbers that add up

    Escalda · wheat market · 2003-09
    Price, per tonne212.0+4.1%
    Imports, kt1,840-6.3%
    Stocks, weeks of use5.2-0.8

    Hover or focus a number. The ring fills for 0.6 s, then the tooltip locks and its underlined terms open a nested tooltip. Esc or moving away closes them, innermost first.

    Lock ring (idea 15). Crusader Kings III and Victoria 3 let a tooltip become a small window you can move into, so terms inside it can open their own tooltips. The cue that it is about to lock is what makes it discoverable.

    Breakdown (idea 16). Europa Universalis IV lists every modifier behind a number. In Ceteris the breakdown is the accounting: price as cost, freight, tariff and margin, quantities as sources and uses, each line opening the explainer.

    Direction colour (idea 17). The changes on the left are blue when up and umber when down. Neither means good. Whether dearer wheat is good depends on who you are, and 1.0 has no seat.

    Seen and left out

    Paradox cues we skip

    • Parchment, brass and wax-seal panels · the Paradox Cabinet look, option B, not chosen
    • Terra incognita and fog of war · fog of data is a game mechanic, 2.0+, and not drawn as clouds
    • Scripted flavour events with options · railroading; events come only from the run (ADR 0241)
    • Painted event illustrations · no commissioned art; 2.0+ at most
    • Portraits on every notice · decided against (ADRs 0183 to 0187)
    • Achievements, missions, journal objectives · collections rejected; objectives are 2.0+
    • 3D building and unit models · flat atlas pictograms instead (ideas 5, 22)
    • Country shield in the top bar · seat chrome, 2.0+ (ADR 0140)
    Cost

    What the 1.0 set would cost

    Most 1.0 items are shader uniforms, sprites or Slint panels over data the engine already produces. Two need real plumbing: curved names (Slint labels cannot follow a path, so names move to a glyph atlas in wgpu, which revisits the label half of ADR 0244) and the breakdown tooltip (every displayed quantity needs a decomposition the explainer can serve).
