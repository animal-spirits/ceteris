Ceteris research note · 28 October 2026

Borrowed mechanics

Twenty mechanics from other games that Ceteris does not already plan, each mapped onto the rule that the sim owns consequences and the game changes state only through interventions. Most of the strongest ones fit the economy-only 1.0 because they are views or lab tools, not new behaviour.

Deduped against DESIGN.md, ADRs 0001-0144, the bold-ideas doc and the three research notes War kept light (separate thread)

Shortlist

Best value for 1.0

These six are cheap, need nothing beyond P2-P5, write no state, and fix the risk the Victoria 3 comparison named first: legibility.

01Kerbal Space Program (delta-v readout, maneuver preview)

Headroom gauges

Fit 1.0Cost S-MView

What it is

KSP shows how much delta-v each stage has left, so the player knows before launch whether the rocket reaches orbit.

Why it works

It turns a hidden physical budget into one number with a direction, so failure is foreseeable instead of sudden.

In Ceteris

Per state, the distance to each switch the model already contains: months of reserves to the peg-break region (9.2), debt and spread to the crisis region (9.13), bank capital to the run threshold (9.11), food stocks to famine rules (0029). Burn rate from ADR 0096 tangents. Where the switch is a hazard, show the hazard curve, not a cliff.

New. Alerts (23.8) fire after a user-set line is crossed; this shows the model's own lines before they are reached.

02Factorio (production statistics), Anno 1800 (supply and demand per good)

Bottleneck lens

Fit 1.0Cost S-MView

What it is

Factorio graphs production and consumption per item over switchable windows (minutes to hundreds of hours). Players trace a starved assembler back up the chain to the missing input.

Why it works

The fun of a production game is finding the binding constraint. A good lens makes the hunt fast without solving it for you.

In Ceteris

A map mode colouring cells where putty-clay rationing (ADR 0020) binds this tick, and a panel that walks the shortfall upstream through the IO table and trade shares to the origin market. Every series gets Factorio's window toggle (month, year, decade).

New. The receipt (0124) traces a household basket; this traces a firm's missing input. Rationing is already computed, so the cost is the view.

03Cities: Skylines and its traffic mods (route view on a road segment)

Who uses this route

Fit 1.0Cost SView

What it is

Select a road segment and every vehicle on it is traced to its origin and destination, so the player sees why a junction jams.

Why it works

It links a local symptom to the distant choices that cause it, which is the whole idea of a network.

In Ceteris

Click a transport edge, port or chokepoint and list the origin-destination pairs and goods routed through it, from the route-choice shares of ADR 0017. A "close this edge" button forks a branch, so the preview and the shock are one gesture.

New. The cartogram (0124) shows costs, not who carries what; chokepoints exist as data but have no per-edge inspector.

04Democracy 4 (the policy web)

Generated influence web

Fit 1.0Cost MView

What it is

Policies, statistics and voter groups are nodes; lines show who affects whom, and hovering a policy lights up everything it touches.

Why it works

A complex system feels learnable when its wiring is visible. Democracy's weakness is that the wiring is authored and fixed.

In Ceteris

Draw the web from the model: levers (tariffs, taxes, policy rate) to intermediate variables to pop groups and sectors, edge weights from ADR 0096 derivatives at the current state, refreshed quarterly. Edges change as the economy changes, and each opens the explainer. Label it local and path-conditional.

New. Listed under "games worth studying" in the design notes but never designed. The leverage map (0117) is spatial and per outcome; this is per lever.

05Budget Hero, CRFB Debt Fixer, Rule the Waves (designing under a budget)

Static and dynamic score

Fit 1.0Cost MLab tool

What it is

Budget games hand the player a menu of policy cards, each with an official cost, to hit a deficit target. Rule the Waves asks for a ship that fits a budget, with every trade-off visible while you design.

Why it works

Live feedback while composing makes trade-offs tangible before committing.

In Ceteris

A package designer for tax, tariff and transfer changes. While editing: the static score (accounting cost at current quantities, as a conventional CBO score) and a per-pop-group incidence table. On "run": the dynamic score from the ensemble under CRN. The package saves as an edit list.

New. Surrogate sliders (0079) predict outcomes; this separates the accounting answer from the general-equilibrium one, which teaches the core idea of the model.

06Victoria 3 (Lessons), Into the Breach

Lessons as small experiments

Fit 1.0Cost MOnboarding

What it is

Victoria 3 opens a short, step-by-step lesson the first time the player meets a mechanic, using the player's own game.

Why it works

Teaching at the moment of need, on the player's own situation, sticks better than a front-loaded tutorial.

In Ceteris

The first price spike, rationing event or capital-flow reversal offers a lesson that forks the user's world, changes one thing (no export ban, half the tariff) and shows the diff under CRN. The user learns the explainer and branch manager by using them. Lessons are text files; experiments are edit lists.

New. The specs have no onboarding. Classroom mode (research notes) is instructor-led; this is self-serve.

07Football Manager (Data Hub)

Peer radar

Fit 1.0Cost SView

What it is

Players' and teams' metrics are shown as percentiles against a chosen comparison group, often as radar charts.

Why it works

Raw numbers mean little without a reference. Percentiles against a fair peer set answer "is this good?" at a glance.

In Ceteris

Any state's headline series as percentiles against a peer set the user picks (income group, region, commodity exporters, currency regime), with the AI-baseline branch as a ghost outline. Peer sets are saved queries.

New. History's percentile (0118) ranks reality within the ensemble; this ranks a state among states.

08Crusader Kings III (game concepts), Civilopedia

In-app pedia from the specs

Fit 1.0Cost S-MView

What it is

In CK3 every game term inside a tooltip is a link to its concept entry; Civ keeps a full encyclopedia one click away.

Why it works

Players learn vocabulary in place instead of leaving the game for a wiki.

In Ceteris

Terms in tooltips (Armington elasticity, putty-clay, iceberg cost) link to pages generated from the specs: the equation, live parameter values with sources, the deciding ADR. The parameter wiki (0127) becomes its parameter section.

Extends 0127 (a static site) into the app's tooltips.

09Factorio (alt-mode), Europa Universalis IV (macro builder)

Numbers on the map and edits by query

Fit 1.0Cost SView, editor

What it is

Holding Alt in Factorio prints contents and recipes over every machine. EU4's macro builder applies one action to every province matching a filter.

Why it works

Colour shows patterns, numbers settle arguments. Bulk actions keep large worlds manageable.

In Ceteris

Hold a key to print the active map mode's value on each province. Select provinces by query ("every port province with a food deficit") and apply one edit to all; the selection is stored with the edit so it replays.

New as UI. Edits by query may already be possible in the scripting API (14.8); this exposes it in the app.

10Transport Tycoon and OpenTTD, Victoria 3 (construction queue)

Corridor planner seat

Fit 1.xCost MSeat, lab candidate

What it is

The player lays track or builds ports and watches cargo find the new route. Victoria 3 adds the wait: projects queue on limited construction capacity.

Why it works

One visible build reshapes flows across the map, which makes network economics feel physical.

In Ceteris

A seat that builds one corridor (canal, rail line, port, pipeline) as an edit on the transport graph. Delivery delay and cost overrun are drawn from reference-class distributions by project type (Flyvbjerg, Holm and Buhl 2002: rail about 45% over, roads about 20%). Score: market-access gains (5.5, Donaldson and Hornbeck 2016) against cost, under CRN.

New seat. 5.5 lets AI states propose projects but has no overrun or delay model; adding one is a small lab change that the seat then uses.

11Mount & Blade (caravans), Offworld Trading Company

Commodity trader seat

Fit 1.xCost MSeat

What it is

In Mount & Blade the player buys cheap in one town and sells dear in another. In Offworld every purchase pushes the shared price up and every sale pushes it down.

Why it works

Arbitrage teaches price geography directly, and price impact punishes greed.

In Ceteris

A trading house that buys, stores and ships between province markets. Small positions are price-taking; past a size the storage rule of 0091 shows the player's own price impact. Scenarios: 2007-08 rice, 2010 wheat, 2022 gas. Uses only the trade core.

Close to the supply-chain manager and macro-fund seats (research notes), but physical arbitrage plus storage is a distinct loop. A good first seat that needs no politics.

12Europa Universalis V (situations), Victoria 3 (journal), Democracy 4 (situations)

Situations as tracked processes

Fit 1.0-1.3Cost MView

What it is

A long multi-actor process (the Hundred Years' War, a housing crisis) gets its own panel with participants, progress and end conditions.

Why it works

Slow processes become legible stories with a beginning and an end, instead of a scatter of events.

In Ceteris

Situations are detectors on model variables with hysteresis (enter at one level, leave at another, so they do not flicker): a peg under pressure, a restructuring, a commodity boom, a sudden stop. Each has a panel, participants and a named arc. Unlike Democracy 4's, they add no effects.

Builds on event log v2 and its detectors (DF path) and chronicle naming (0078). The persistent tracking panel is new.

13Old World (ambitions), EU4 missions without the script

Generated ambitions

Fit 1.3Cost S-MGame layer

What it is

Old World periodically offers a few goals suited to the player's state; picking and completing one earns legitimacy.

Why it works

Short-horizon goals give a sandbox direction without scripting history.

In Ceteris

Every few game years, offer three objectives built from the seat's data (gaps against peers, open situations): "inflation under 10% by 2004". Each shows its difficulty as the share of AI-baseline lanes that reach it. A chosen ambition joins the 23.4 objective vector with a fixed weight.

New. 23.4 has preset and user-written objectives; none are generated or priced by the ensemble.

14Opus Magnum and other Zachtronics games (solution histograms)

Pareto histograms

Fit 1.3Cost SScoring view

What it is

After a solve, the game shows your solution on histograms of every player's cost, cycles and area. There is no single score.

Why it works

Several axes invite replay to improve one at the expense of another, and show trade-offs without a weighting.

In Ceteris

At the end of a scenario, place the result on histograms per axis (welfare, debt, emissions, bottom-40 income) against the AI-baseline ensemble, the player's earlier runs, and any runs shared by hash through the timeline library (0132). No server needed.

New. 23.4 scores one weighted objective; this shows the whole frontier beside it.

15Slay the Spire (Ascension)

Ascension ladder

Fit 1.3Cost SGame layer

What it is

Twenty numbered difficulty levels, each adding one modifier and unlocked by winning the level below.

Why it works

Mastery has a visible path, and each step changes one thing the player can name.

In Ceteris

Order 23.4's honest frictions into rungs: reporting lag, statistical fog (0065), revisions, markets that learn the player's rule faster, a longer AI planning horizon. No rung bends the model.

Packaging of 23.4 difficulty, which is already defined but has no progression.

16Kerbal Space Program (maneuver nodes)

Policy paths on the timeline

Fit 1.3Cost S-MEditor

What it is

Drop a node on the orbit, drag its handles, and the predicted trajectory redraws before the burn.

Why it works

Planning becomes direct manipulation with instant prediction, then a deliberate commit.

In Ceteris

Drag nodes onto the timeline to draft a lever path (a rate path, a tariff phase-in). The surrogate fan redraws as nodes move; "commit" turns nodes into timed edits and launches the full ensemble to confirm.

Extends surrogate sliders (0079) from one value to a schedule.

17Frostpunk (irreversible laws), Stellaris (policy cooldowns)

Commitment devices

Fit 1.3Cost MLab candidate

What it is

Frostpunk's laws cannot be repealed; Stellaris locks a policy for years after a change. Choices bind the future.

Why it works

Irreversibility gives decisions weight and stops the player from toggling every month.

In Ceteris

Real versions with real payoffs: a fiscal rule, a currency board (0123), central-bank independence, a WTO tariff binding. Binding removes levers or adds an exit cost; in return, expectations (9.13) put more weight on the announced rule. This is the Kydland-Prescott trade of flexibility for credibility. Weight and its evidence (for example Iara and Wolff 2014 on fiscal rules and spreads) need a 17 entry.

New mechanism. 0083 covers announcements moving expectations before implementation, not binding.

18Europa Universalis V (proximity and control)

State reach

Fit 2.0Cost MLab candidate

What it is

Control over a location falls with its travel distance from the capital, which caps what the player can extract there.

Why it works

Geography constrains power, so roads and administrative seats matter beyond trade.

In Ceteris

Tax collection and policy implementation (0083) fall with generalized trade cost (5.4) from the capital or regional seats; infrastructure raises reach. Evidence: Michalopoulos and Papaioannou (2014) on national institutions fading with distance from the capital; Herbst (2000). Feeds 0067 state capacity.

New. 0067 and 0068 have no distance term. Institutions are fixed in 1.0, so this waits for 2.0.

19Frostpunk 2 (council votes), Suzerain

Whip count

Fit 2.0Cost MGame layer

What it is

Before a vote, Frostpunk 2 shows how each faction will vote, and the player can trade promises to flip them.

Why it works

Legislation becomes a negotiation with visible stakes, and broken promises come back later.

In Ceteris

For a bill in the legislative process (10.6), show the projected count by party and interest group with intervals, and what would move each. Promises are recorded commitments; breaking one costs trust with that group (10.7).

New UI over P6. The coalition-partner seat (0130) uses the same machinery.

20Tropico (El Presidente's Swiss account)

Private ledger objective

Fit 2.0Cost SObjective preset

What it is

The ruler can skim into a personal account, and the score counts it apart from the island's welfare.

Why it works

Two ledgers make the conflict between ruler and ruled a choice the player feels.

In Ceteris

An objective preset scoring the ruling coalition's offshore wealth (profit shifting and havens, 0041; corruption, 10.1) against veil-of-ignorance welfare (0129). The post-mortem shows both.

New preset on existing scoring. Needs 10.1 to move, so 2.0.

War, briefly

Left to the war thread. Two pointers for it: Rule the Waves sets the naval budget from international tension and hides rival designs until they are seen in battle, which suits the dossier rule; Shadow Empire draws supply as a network flow from headquarters with truck capacity, which matches the transport graph. Mobilization through the IO table is already the quartermaster seat (0130).

Every plate respects one rule: views write no state, and seats act only through the Steward action space as recorded edits.
