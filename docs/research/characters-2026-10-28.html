Ceteris · proposal · 28 October 2026 · not an ADR yet

People who remember, faces that age

Yes, portraits can be generated procedurally. Every face here comes from about 30 numbers and 9 choices, composited from layered parts into a framed, lit bust in the manner of Crusader Kings II, aged by the game date, and stored as nothing more than a seed. The character system behind them can stay thin. People hold offices, carry traits read from public data and a short memory, and act only by choosing among actions their office already has, so the simulation gains a leader time scale without a single scripted event.

One choice is yours. DESIGN 23.5 and ADR 0066 say characters never carry real names. A start on 1 January 2000 reads far better with Cardoso, Schröder and Putin in office, and with their successors generated. My recommendation and its safeguards are in Real people and generated successors.

Working demo

The portrait bench

Twelve generated people for one office and country. Pick one to see the same face from 1 January 2000 to 2060, a strip at four ages, a dossier with what they remember from real history up to 2000, and the genome that draws them. Changing the seed changes the people; the same seed always gives the same faces.

These parts are drawn in code as placeholders. CK2's look comes from hand-painted part sprites (heads, eyes, noses, mouths, hair, beards, clothes) composited by the same kind of genome. Shipping quality needs a painted library of about 150 parts in a few skin and age variants, commissioned or drafted by an image model and repainted by hand, never generated per character. The genome, ageing, determinism and storage below carry over unchanged. The framed, painted plate is a portrait-only exception to the flat Modern Atlas panels of ADR 0137.

Country sets only skin tone, hair texture and colour, eye colour and names. Face shape is drawn from the same distribution for everyone. "From data" uses an illustrative 30% share of women; the real generator reads the share for the office, country and year (WhoGov, IPU). Pigment priors here are hand-set placeholders, not calibrated values.

Who starts real

      
    
        
  • Incumbents on 1 January 2000: heads of state and government, cabinets, central bank governors, party leaders, service chiefs. Positions and dates from Wikidata (CC0, position held with start and end), cabinets from WhoGov, entry manner and earlier tenures from Archigos and REIGN.
    • 
        
  • The bench: people in public life in 2000 who could plausibly rise, such as national legislators, governors, capital mayors and deputy ministers. Many real 2000s leaders were on such a bench in 2000.
    • 
        
  • Coverage is uneven: Wikidata knows far more about Swedish deputies than about Chadian ones. Every state's bench is filled to the same size with generated people, so documentation does not become political advantage.
    • 
      

    

    

      
What the simulation owns

      
    
        
  • No real dates after 2000. A real person ages under the life tables of 13.5 and leaves office by the hazards of ADR 0066. Their real death, defeat or scandal never fires by date. Real post-2000 careers are validation targets (0107), never inputs.
    • 
        
  • No hindsight in traits. Latent traits of real people come only from priors on what was public before 2000: age, education, military record, party, earlier record in office. Nobody hand-sets "risk-loving" for a named leader; that would be both defamatory and a leak of the holdout.
    • 
        
  • Everyone later is generated, materialised from the pop cells that would have produced them (see Careers). Generated names are checked against Wikidata's living notable people and redrawn on a match.
    • 
      

    

  

  

    
Decision needed: real names

    
DESIGN 23.5 says characters are "generated people with generic names, never real ones". Three ways forward:

    
    
      
  • recommended Real 2000 roster, generated after. Real names and public facts for incumbents and the bench; generated everyone else. Extended profile shows names; the lab shows role labels ("Head of government, Brazil") so papers never cite a fictional Cardoso. A run-level "Real names" switch can turn every real person into a generated stand-in with the same public facts.
    • 
      
  • Real facts, generated names. The current spec: 2000 offices are seeded from real records, but names and faces are invented.
    • 
      
  • Fully generated. Real records only as validation data.
    • 
    

    
With real names, the simulation will show real living people doing things they never did, including orders under ADR 0154. Safeguards: chronicle exports and postcards carry a "counterfactual run" line; real people never get a generated face (next section); shared timelines (0132) keep the run's names setting so a viewer can switch it.

Traits grounded in data

    
Observables come from data and are visible. Latent traits are drawn from priors conditioned on those observables and stay hidden, seen only through the dossier signals of 23.5. Character system · 3 of 6

    
Memory

    
This is the gap the Dwarf Fortress comparison named. A character's memory is small, typed and deterministic, and it acts only through parameters that already exist.

  

  

    

      
What a character holds

      
    
        
  • Lived macro experience: the declining-weight kernel of Malmendier and Nagel over inflation, unemployment, war and crisis years. This is ADR 0139's experience kernel at the level of one person, so the bench demo's inflation bar is the same mechanism.
    • 
        
  • Episodes: the 16 most salient events the person took part in (a coup survived, an election lost, a war commanded, a purge, an exile), each an event id from the log, a counterpart, a valence and a tick. Salience decays; new episodes push out the weakest.
    • 
        
  • Relations: typed edges (patron, protégé, ally, rival, betrayed by, purged by), created only by logged events such as an appointment, a coalition break or a dismissal.
    • 
      

    

    

      
What memory may change

      
    
        
  • Expectations and hawkishness, through the experience kernel.
    • 
        
  • Coalition bargaining costs (10.5) between people with a grudge, and the chance of joining a coup against someone who purged you.
    • 
        
  • Risk aversion after a lost war or a near-miss crisis.
    • 
        
  • Nothing else. With memory weights at zero, the model reproduces ADR 0066 bit for bit, which is the nesting test.
    • 
      

      
Cost: about 200 bytes per person. With about 30 office holders and 150 bench members per state, roughly 36,000 people are live at once and about 150,000 over a 50-year run, around 30 MB.

    

  






  

    
Character system · 4 of 6

    
Careers

    
Offices are slots in a graph per state; people move between them by actions and hazards, and new people enter from the population rather than from a random table.

  

  

    

      
    
        
  • Office graph per state from the constitution (Comparative Constitutions Project, V-Dem): head of state, head of government, portfolios, central bank, party leaderships, service chiefs and HQ commanders (ADR 0147), governors.
    • 
        
  • Pools feed the graph: party ranks, legislature, civil service, officer corps (ADR 0146), business owners, academia, protest leaders (10.8).
    • 
        
  • Moves: an appointment is an action of the appointer, scored by the loyalty and competence trade-off; elections pick party leaders; officers rise by merit or patronage, and medals inflate with patronage (23.5); exits follow scandal, health, purge and defeat hazards.
    • 
      

    

    

      
    
        
  • Lazy materialisation: a 2035 prime minister was born around 1975 and exists in 2000 only as part of a pop cell. A person is instantiated when they first enter a pool, with a biography backdated from that cell's history (birth year, region, schooling, what they lived through).
    • 
        
  • Composition is emergent: who reaches office depends on the pop cells that feed each pool and on the sim's selection (education, class, region, group access to power from EPR). The generator puts no ethnic or gender quota on offices; under-representation has to come from mechanisms and is checked against WhoGov and IPU shares.
    • 
        
  • Names from Wikidata given-name and family-name frequencies by birth region, with Fluent grammars for order and form (family-first, patronymics, double surnames), reusing the ADR 0143 tooling.
    • 
      

    

  






  

    
Character system · 5 of 6

    
Characters act only through interventions

    
A character has no verbs of its own. Traits and memory set the parameters of the planner that already chooses for the office; the choice is written to the intervention list like any player action, with the character as author.

  

  

    
Traits and memoryObservables, latent draws, experience kernel, relations

    
→

    
Planner parametersWeights, noise, Taylor coefficients, slippage, risk

    
→

    
Office's action spaceThe same menu a player in that seat has

    
→

    
Intervention listAction with author = character id, tick, seed stream

    
→

    
Simulation and event logConsequences; events write episodes and relations back

  

  
    
    
  • A player in a seat replaces the planner, not the person. A minister you appoint still carries their slippage, so appointments matter to players too.
    • 
    
  • Appointments, dismissals and purges are actions as well; births, deaths and memory updates are logged events. Replays and forks need nothing new.
    • 
    
  • Character RNG streams are keyed by (entity id, purpose), following the entity-ID rule of 14.4, so two branches keep the same people until their histories differ. That is what the alternate-lives view of ADR 0132 needs.
    • 
    
  • Generals are characters on the same record. ADR 0147 already lists "P6 characters" as its dependency; dossiers, latency modifiers and order adjustment by risk aversion attach there.
    •

Lab and extended profile

    
Per ADR 0136, characters ship in the extended profile with P6 (2.0-alpha) and become a lab default at 4.0 only if they pass ablation (15.7). The lab keeps the stochastic leader attributes until then, and the nesting test guarantees the two agree when traits sit at role means and memory weights are zero.

Portraits · 2 of 4

    
Real leaders and plausibility without caricature

  

  

    

      
Real people

      
    
        
  • No generated likeness of a real person, procedural or AI. A drawn "likeness" of a real leader is a caricature by construction, and an AI one is a deepfake of a public figure; most providers refuse them anyway.
    • 
        
  • Real people get a plate: monogram, name, office and dates, in the seat's letterhead style (ADR 0141), as in the Brazil columns above.
    • 
        
  • Optional Commons photo in the dossier only, never on event cards beside counterfactual acts, with author, licence and year.
    • 
        
  • The "Real names" switch replaces each real person with a generated stand-in, who then gets a procedural face.
    • 
      

    

    

      
Rules for generated faces

      
    
        
  • Geometry is independent of origin. Head, jaw, eyes, lids, nose and lips come from one distribution for everyone; no part exists only for one group.
    • 
        
  • Origin may set only skin tone, hair texture, hair colour and eye colour, each a broad overlapping distribution.
    • 
        
  • Pigment is a property of a person's ancestral cell, carried by migration (section 6), and never a lookup from an EPR group name, in line with ADR 0141's ban on ethnicity as a skin.
    • 
        
  • Dress by office and era, never by identity: suits, uniforms, open collars. No costume, and no religious dress unless the office's own protocol carries it (an open question for clerical offices).
    • 
        
  • Lint, as for flags: a contrast check, a cap on extreme parameter combinations, and a review of the parts library by people from the regions it draws.
    •

Age progression

    
Age is not stored in the face. The renderer takes the genome and the age on the game date, so one face covers a whole career. Each effect has its own onset and rate in the genome, which is why two people of the same age look different.

Portraits · 4 of 4

    
Determinism and storage

  

  

    

      
    
        
  • Store the seed, not the image. Face parameters derive from a hash of (character id, "face"), outside the simulation's RNG, so faces cost no state and changing the generator never moves a state hash.
    • 
        
  • Only four inputs are state: birth date, sex, ancestral cell and office. The renderer is a pure function of those, the face hash and the date.
    • 
        
  • Versioned generator. Faces can change between app versions (a better nose part). The app version is already in the run hash rule of ADR 0128, and the face hash is printed under every portrait.
    • 
      

    

    

      
    
        
  • Edits are edits. A player who adjusts a face or accepts a model's genome stores the changed numbers (about 100 bytes) as an edit in the run file (14.2), exactly like a chosen flag or name.
    • 
        
  • Raster portraits by hash. A commissioned image goes to a side asset store under its content hash; the edit references the hash; shared runs (0128, 0132) carry the asset. Replays never call a model.
    • 
        
  • Cache rendered textures by (face hash, age in years, generator version); a 200 by 240 portrait renders in well under a millisecond with resvg.
    •
