Ceteris Paribus · design note · 28 Oct 2026

Clocks, hours and frames

Can war run hour by hour like Hearts of Iron, what else can we do with time, and how do we keep the clock smooth and the frame rate stable at maximum speed. Grounded in ADRs 0088, 0097, 0132, 0145, 0148, 0149, 0188, 0196 and 0207, and the measured 34.9 ms econ tick. Numbers marked "est." are estimates; benchmarks replace them.

Short answer
  • Hourly war: yes, but sparse. Keep the day as the commit step (snapshots, hashes, the supply solve, planners, intel). Inside each day, run an hourly phase only for the cheap, fast processes on engaged segments: movement, contact, combat rounds, air sorties, naval interception. Uniform hourly ticks would cost 24 times the daily war budget and drop a world war to about 1.5 simulated days per second.
  • Most of the "HOI feel" is presentation, not physics. A clock that visibly moves hour by hour and units that glide come from interpolation between ticks. That works at peace too, on the monthly tick.
  • Smoothness comes from one structural choice: the simulation runs ahead of what you see, on its own threads, and the screen plays back a buffer of finished ticks at a steady rate. Determinism makes this free of risk: an edit lands at the view tick (0188), and the ticks computed past it are dropped and recomputed bit for bit the same way they would have been.
  • Placement. The presentation pipeline belongs to the App shell phase (1.0). Hourly war is a P7 (2.0) amendment to 0145 and 0149, both still proposed.
1 · War hour by hour

Three clocks become four, but the fourth runs only where fighting is

0145 already nests a daily sub-tick inside the monthly tick, active only while a war is on. HOI ticks every hour everywhere, which it can afford because its per-hour work is tiny. Ceteris's daily war step is heavier (a supply min-cost flow, delegated planners, belief updates), so hours must not multiply that work. The split below keeps the expensive layers daily and gives hours only to processes whose timing actually changes outcomes within a day.

What hours buy, concretely

  • Naval interception. A task group at 25 knots covers about 1,100 km a day, more than most of the 725 sea zones. Daily resolution cannot tell whether two fleets met in a zone or passed through it. This is the strongest reason for hours.
  • Air sortie cycles of 3 to 6 hours, day and night operations, and weather that moves through the day.
  • Order latency in hours. 0147's HQ latency is naturally hours at brigade and division level; rounding to whole days overstates it.
  • Breakthrough and exploitation inside a day: a pursuit state that opens at 06:00 can carry 40 km by nightfall instead of waiting for the next day.
  • Strategic strikes with flight times of minutes to hours (0152) get an exact hour of impact.

Determinism and replay under option B

  • Time inside a day is an integer hour. No floating time anywhere, so ordering never depends on rounding.
  • The hourly queue is a timing wheel of 24 slots (19.4 already uses one for shipments). Within a slot, events run in a fixed order: kind priority, then entity ID.
  • Draw keys gain an hour: (run seed, war, entity, day, hour, purpose). Untouched entities keep their streams across forks, so CRN still holds.
  • Snapshots stay at day boundaries during war. Hourly state is transient and always reconstructible by replaying one day from its start, which costs milliseconds.
  • Orders and edits carry (day, hour). The intervention list sorts by them; Tier 1a actions are unchanged.
  • Nesting test: a segment with constant inputs stepped 24 times hourly must match the daily closed form within a stated tolerance, so the lab profile (daily, aggregate) still nests the extended one.
  • A run with no war still never enters any sub-tick, and its hash still equals a build without ceteris-war.

3 · Other ways to play with time

What determinism and the run log make cheap

Speeds in sim time, not ticks

App shell · 1.0

Speed 1 = one day per second, 2 = a week, 3 = a month, 4 = a quarter, 5 = as fast as the machine goes. The feel stays the same at peace and at war; when war makes a speed unreachable, the clock eases down to what the simulation sustains and shows a "sim-bound" chip. 20.3 keeps five speeds and 0188's keys.

Continuous clock at peace

App shell · 1.0

The date counts days between monthly ticks and map fills cross-fade from one month to the next. Panels always show tick values, never interpolated ones, and say which tick they show.

Scrub the past without resimulating

P3 · 1.0

History columns already hold every map mode at every tick. Dragging the timeline back reads history, not state, so it is instant. Resuming or forking from a scrubbed tick loads the nearest snapshot and replays at most 11 ticks (about 0.4 s), or instantly from a ring of recent tick deltas kept with P3's copy-on-write chunks.

Think ahead while paused

App shell or P3 · 1.0

While paused or reading a panel, idle cores keep computing the next ticks. Pressing play is instant. Any edit at the view tick throws away ticks past it; determinism guarantees the kept ones are exactly what a fresh run would produce.

Auto-pace around events

1.x minor

The presented clock slows when salient events cluster (war declared, a front breaks, a bank run) and returns to speed in quiet stretches, extending 0188's watchers. In seat play it uses only what the seat can see and slows at the event, never before it, so the run-ahead buffer cannot leak the future.

Replay the day

P7 · 2.0

With hourly events logged, any past day of a battle can be watched hour by hour as an animation, at no simulation cost. This replaces the continuous battle view that 0149 turned down.

Run to next event, skip quiet stretches

App shell · 1.0

Run to the next alert, situation change or date with rendering throttled to a progress view, so every core but one serves the simulation. Worth roughly 10 to 15% over speed 5 (est.).

Branches side by side

P3 to 1.x

Baseline and fork run on split core sets with one shared presented clock; a pinned window (0196) shows each. A continuous forecast cone ahead of the clock is 20.3's "ensemble from here" kept running while paused.

Day and night, local time

P7 · 2.0

With hours, the map can draw the terminator and local times, and night penalties become visible rather than abstract. Cosmetic at peace, mechanical at war.

Real-time crises

already in 0132

"Thirteen days" pacing for nuclear standoffs, bank runs and coups now runs on hours instead of days, so wall-clock deadlines on event cards line up with the in-game clock.

Considered and set aside: slowing one region while others run (one world has one clock; replay the day instead); overlapping a month's war days with the next month's economy (needs a one-month lag in war's effects on the economy, a model change); optimistic parallel simulation with rollback (Time Warp) and Parareal (both rejected earlier for the econ core on cost and complexity).

4 · Smooth time, stable frames

The simulation never draws, and the screen never waits

The core rule: the frame loop never calls into a tick, and a tick never waits on a frame. Everything below follows from it, and none of it touches state, so run hashes do not move.

01

Fixed step for the sim, vsync for the screen

The simulation advances in fixed ticks on its own pool; the frame loop runs at display refresh and only reads. This is the classic "fix your timestep" pattern, with Ceteris's ticks as the fixed step. ADR 0088's map-as-texture through Slint's rendering notifier already fits it.

02

Run-ahead ring with a rate governor

A small ring (2 to 4 ticks, or about half a second of presented time) absorbs uneven tick costs: a 35 ms peace month next to a 900 ms world-war month. The presented clock moves at the chosen speed and is eased toward what the ring can sustain, so a slow month shows as a gentle slow-down, never a hitch.

03

Interpolate on the GPU

Upload the two bracketing ticks' values once per tick and a single interpolation factor per frame; the fill shader mixes them through the feature colour texture of 0010 and 0011. Per-frame CPU cost is near zero. Units move along their path between hourly or daily positions as instanced sprites from a symbol atlas (NATO symbols at operational zoom, 0166).

04

Upload only what changed

About 4.1 million land cells at H3 resolution 6 (est.) carry war control, but a day changes a few thousand. Send a list of (cell, value) pairs and scatter them with a compute pass. Front lines rebuild only for changed segments.

05

Give each thread a core class

On an M4 Pro: the frame loop at user-interactive priority on a performance core; the sim pool on the remaining performance cores, not all of them; history compression, snapshot writes and ensemble lanes at utility priority, where macOS places them on efficiency cores. Separate pools, so an ensemble can never steal the frame loop's core.

06

Nothing slow on the frame loop

No allocation, file I/O or locks while drawing. Shader pipelines compiled and cached at start, the glyph atlas warmed, pack files memory-mapped. Panel models diff visible rows only (0088). Frame time measured with GPU timestamps; the target is the 99th percentile under 8.3 ms, not the mean.

07

Sleep what is quiet

Sim side: no war means no sub-tick (0145); reserves and quiet fronts wake on events; rare events use sampled next-event times (19.4). Tick cost then tracks what is happening, not the size of the world.

08

Overlap tick tails

Writing history and snapshots for tick t runs on a background thread while tick t+1 computes; it only reads t's published view. The 0097 typed, compressed history makes that write small.

09

Theatres in parallel

0145 already allows theatres with no shared supply edge or front to run concurrently, reduced in theatre-ID order. This is the main lever for world wars, since the econ tick's parallelism across goods is spent (0097).
