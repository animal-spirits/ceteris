How Ceteris should model companies

Keep companies as a closed-form distribution of firms inside each enterprise cell, and give that distribution real work to do. The first job is to fix the welfare bug the P2 benchmark found. Then add an ownership axis from OECD data, entry and exit with a zombie band, and the state-firm rules already proposed. Named firms such as Toyota or Gazprom come later, first as labels and then as an opt-in.

The proposal in one screen

Five layers, cheapest first

A company in Ceteris is a slice of a Pareto distribution of firms inside each (province, sector, ownership) cell. The distribution is solved in closed form, so it costs a few numbers per cell and per trade link. Explicit firm agents stay out of the lab default until they beat the closed form out of sample, which is the bar DESIGN 15.7 already sets.

Selection on every trade linkExporters to a destination are the cell's best firms. One multiplier per link turns this into delivered quantities, buyers pay the Eaton-Kortum price index, and the ACR welfare formula holds. This fixes the P2 benchmark failure.

Selection is the missing piece behind the ACR failure

L1 · the benchmark gap

The P2 benchmark found that buyers pay q_d = 1/alpha_d, with alpha_d = sum_o pi_od / c_od. That is a spending-weighted harmonic mean of delivered costs. When a border cost rises, spending moves away from the dearer source, and this mean can fall. That is why Japan gains from costlier borders, the EU gains from Brexit, and the ACR formula fails.

What is missing is selection. In Eaton-Kortum, a country buys from a far source only the goods that source is unusually good at, so the average price paid per source is the same for every source and equals the price index P_d. In Melitz-Chaney the same thing happens through firms: only a cell's most productive firms clear the fixed and variable cost of serving a distant market. The engine spreads spending with EK shares but then charges buyers each source's average cost, as if the goods shipped were average goods.

EK: pi_od = K_od c_od^-theta / Phi_d P_d = Gamma * Phi_d^(-1/theta) selection multiplier on link od: s_od = c_od / P_d = Gamma^-1 (K_od / pi_od)^(1/theta) alpha_d' = sum_o pi_od s_od / c_od = 1 / P_d so q_d = P_d exactly Melitz-Chaney (Pareto shape k, CES sigma, fixed cost f_od): share of cell o's firms serving d n_od = (phi_min / phi*_od)^k mean efficiency of those firms phi~_od = (k / (k - sigma + 1))^(1/(sigma-1)) * phi*_od s_od proportional to n_od^(-1/k) (same form as EK, extensive margin in place of pi)

The proposal: every kernel leg carries s_od, computed from shares the kernel already has, at one powf per active leg. Units leave the origin in base units and arrive as s_od times as many delivered units. The gain is booked as origin productivity: the firms serving d need 1/s_od of the cell's average inputs per unit. The goods invariant of 4.6 then holds in delivered units, with selection as one explicit term beside iceberg melt. This also resolves the trade SPEC's objection to P_d, that "a unit bought at P_d delivers fewer than one unit": with s_od applied, it delivers exactly one.

Tests for the ADR: a two-country toy reproduces ACR's W' = lambda_dd'^(-1/epsilon) to machine precision; rerunning the benchmark removes the gains from costlier borders for Japan and from Brexit for the EU; with fixed costs and selection off, hashes equal today's. One open point for the econ owner: under the 0089 calibration, Gamma and the base-unit normalization should be set so that s_dd = 1 at the 2000 data state. Then the calibrated steady state is unchanged, and only counterfactuals move.

How games handle companies

Legibility lessons

Victoria 3

Companies arrived in 1.5 as national boosters. Each one was tied to a set of building types and raised throughput for all of them in the country. A company's Prosperity rose when its earnings per worker beat the global average. It owned no buildings and was not multinational (DD 92). The 1.9 Charters of Commerce update let companies buy building levels and open Regional HQs abroad. Charters (colonization, trade, industry, investment, monopoly) became the player's verbs, and executives became characters whose interest group gains power when the company does well (DD 147).

Lesson: players read companies as named, ownable, political things. Their mechanics still sit on top of the building economy as modifiers, and Ceteris should avoid that: here a named firm must be a part of the cell, never an extra bonus on top of it.

Victoria 2

Factories are owned by capitalist pops or the state. Profits feed pop savings, and unprofitable factories close. Ownership is a pop stratum, and there are no firms as such.

Lesson: the profit-to-owner-to-investment loop of 7.4 and 0104 already covers this.

Capitalism Lab, OpenTTD, Offworld Trading Company

In these games the player is a firm. Capitalism Lab simulates product quality, brand and stock markets. OpenTTD and Offworld let rivals buy you out once you are weak.

Lesson: a firm seat (0130) needs an explicit firm with a balance sheet and an owner, which here means L5's granular firms.

The proposed model

State, rules and cost per layer

L1. Cell distribution and link selection

  • State per cell: firm mass M, lower bound phi_min (absorbed into the cell's A), domestic cutoff phi*_dom. State per active cross-border link (gateway level, country pair by good): cutoff phi*_od, kept as s_od.
  • Fixed export costs f_od (P51) by border crossing, as 0026 has it. They are paid in origin labour, which the labour accounts must count.
  • Outputs at no extra cost: firm count, exporter count and share by destination, mean exporter size, and the size distribution. All are in the inspector and on the scorecard.
  • Cost: about 200 x 200 x 45 links at most, mostly empty. That is one pow per active leg per tick, well inside the P2 budget.

L2. Ownership axis

  • Cells split into domestic private, state and foreign only where a type has at least 5% of cell VA in 2000 (a register threshold). The foreign cell carries a parent-country share vector (top five parents plus rest of world), so dividends, sanctions freezes and 0041 shifting route by parent.
  • Foreign cells sit higher in the tail, following Helpman-Melitz-Yeaple. Their phi_min premium comes from AAMNE VA per worker by ownership. State cells take the 0171 objective.
  • The foreign-owned share moves through the 7.4 logit. Foreign owners see host provinces with an FDI barrier wedge from the OECD FDI Regulatory Restrictiveness Index. AAMNE 2000-2019 then gives an out-of-sample path for the foreign share, a new 15.4 target.
  • This replaces 0104's fixed foreign share f_c,g with state that evolves. Nationalization and privatization (10.4, 2.0) become moves of mass between ownership cells.

L3. Entry, exit and zombies

quarterly, per cell: entry E = eta_e * M * max(0, V_e / (w f_e) - 1) V_e: expected PV of a draw from the tail exit X = delta * M + M * [1 - (phi*_old / phi*_new)^k]+ death plus cutoff exit zombies phi_z = phi*_dom * (1 - z_b) z_b from bank capital gap (0172) zombie share = 1 - (phi_z / phi*_dom)^k , zombies hold inputs and labour, earn below cost
  • The cell's 7.4 exit rule (persistent losses, negative equity) is the cell-wide limit of this. Exit within the cell is continuous instead.
  • With Pareto and free entry, the steady-state firm mass scales with labour and does not depend on trade costs. That gives the equilibrium-nesting test.
  • Cohort vintages (age bins with their own mass, reusing the putty-clay vintage machinery) are an extended-profile follow-up for the Sedlacek-Sterk "lost cohort" after 2008.

L4. Behaviour by ownership

  • 0171 for state cells, 0170 for every cell, 0172 as above, and 0030 behind S14. None changes: L2 gives 0171 the cell it was written for.
  • Leverage per cell feeds the Merton default probability of 9.10. Pareto dispersion also gives a cell a share of financially constrained small firms. That stays a scorecard pattern, not a mechanism, until P5 shows it is needed.

L5. Named firms

  • View (1.x minor). Wikidata (CC0) supplies the real 2000 firms per country and sector: name, headquarters, inception, parent and industry. Rank r in a cell shows the expected r-th order statistic of the cell's tail. Reported 2000 revenues check the tail (the Zipf test) and are never inputs. Named firms change no state, so under 0136 this is a minor. It gives the inspector and the chronicle the names players expect.
  • Granular firms (extended opt-in). The top N firms per cell become explicit agents with idiosyncratic shocks, and the closed-form tail is truncated above them, so aggregates stay consistent. Atkeson-Burstein markups and a firm seat need this layer. It enters the lab only if it beats L1-L4 on out-of-sample GDP volatility.
  • Political actors (2.0). Business groups and large firms become 0189 organizations of the lobby kind, funded from tail profits. Executives come from the 0186 business-owner pool. Protection for sale and state capture act through the 2.0 policy levers.

Interface agreed with the "Design construction and investment" thread: an investment order's owner is an OwnerId. In 1.0 it is always an enterprise cell, so every project is in one province. Granular firms would emit one order per province. Firm entry changes M and places no capacity order.

Four proposed ADRs, waiting for your go

  1. Selection multiplier on trade links. Buyers pay the EK index, and s_od is booked at the origin. This amends 0015, 0026 and the trade SPEC's q_d row. It is a P2 module, so it needs your approval of the spec change before any code.
  2. Ownership cells from OECD AAMNE. Domestic, state and foreign with a parent vector, and an FDI barrier wedge. This amends 3.2, 0104 and 0041.
  3. Entry, exit and the zombie band. Free entry in M, death plus cutoff exit, and 0172 read as an exit cutoff. It adds the Zipf rule for sigma (P50).
  4. Named firms. A view from Wikidata in 1.x, granular firms as an extended opt-in, and political actors deferred to 0189.
