# 0072. International order, peace operations, courts and soft power

Status: proposed
Date: 2026-10-24
Design sections: 11.1, 11.3, 11.8, 10.7
Phase: P6, P7

## Context

Four features of the international system are absent. Peacekeeping reduces violence against
civilians and shortens wars when deployed at scale (Hultman, Kathman and Shannon 2014; Walter,
Howard and Fortna 2021). International criminal courts change leaders' post-tenure prospects
and travel. Attractiveness to foreign publics and elites (soft power: media reach, students,
diaspora, aid) shifts alignment and UN voting. Relative power trajectories make bargaining
failure more likely when a rising state is dissatisfied, a cleaner restatement of
power-transition arguments as commitment problems (Powell 2006).

## Options

1. Status quo.
2. Peace operations as deployable interventions, court indictments as leader-level shocks, a
   soft-power index per state, and shifting-power terms in bargaining.

## Decision

Option 2.

- Peace operations: UN or regional deployments by Security Council or regional decision, with
  troop contributors, lowering civilian violence and recurrence with the size of the force
  (P123).
- Courts: indictments raise a leader's expected post-tenure penalty (0066) and restrict travel;
  states can join or leave the court.
- Soft power per state from media reach, student and diaspora flows, aid (0070) and values
  distance (0055); it shifts foreign elites' and publics' opinion updates and alignment (P124).
- Shifting power: projected relative capability from the growth model enters the
  commitment-problem term of 11.2 rather than a separate power-transition rule.

## Migration

- P6 and P7.
- Data: IPI peacekeeping database and Geo-PKO (terms to audit), UN General Assembly voting data
  (Voeten; terms to audit).

## Tests

- In simulation, larger deployments lower civilian deaths with the sign and rough size of
  Hultman et al.

## Consequences

The international order becomes a set of measurable levers rather than background.
