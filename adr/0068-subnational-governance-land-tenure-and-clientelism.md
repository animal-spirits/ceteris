# 0068. Subnational governance, land tenure, clientelism and electoral integrity

Status: proposed; amended by 0195 (two-tier split becomes a preset over bodies and competences)
Date: 2026-10-24
Design sections: 10.3, 10.4, 10.5, 7.4, 8.7, 11.6
Phase: P6

## Context

Four institutional features with strong evidence are missing or implicit. Decentralization
splits fiscal and administrative authority across tiers, and regional elites act as interest
groups. Formal land tenure raises investment (Besley 1995; Goldstein and Udry 2008), shapes
slum formation and collateral, and insecure tenure drives land conflict such as farmer-herder
violence in the Sahel. Clientelism trades targeted goods for votes and weakens programmatic
policy (Stokes et al. 2013). Electoral manipulation is a menu (repression, fraud, media
control) whose use depends on capacity and costs.

## Options

1. Status quo.
2. Authority shares by tier, a land-tenure security share per province, a clientelism intensity
   per party and state, and an electoral-manipulation menu.

## Decision

Option 2.

- Subnational tier: each state splits revenue and spending authority between national and
  first-level governments by its 2000 federal structure; governors join interest groups (10.4);
  regional capture by organized crime is possible (0060).
- Land tenure security per province raises investment returns (7.4), lowers land conflict
  hazards, enables mortgage collateral (0040), and limits slum formation (0074) (P116).
- Clientelism intensity per party, from V-Dem indicators, diverts spending to targeted
  transfers and lowers the weight of performance in voting.
- Electoral manipulation: incumbents choose repression, fraud or media control when the
  expected vote is short, with costs depending on capacity, international scrutiny and protest
  risk (P117); observed manipulation lowers legitimacy.

## Migration

- P6.
- Data: V-Dem (clientelism, election integrity), Regional Authority Index (licence to audit),

## Consequences

Elections, land and decentralization become levers with documented effects rather than fixed
context.
